## 0. The idea in one breath

**ReCircuit helps city residents decide what to do with an old electronic device (repair it, pass it on, or recycle it) and shows them exactly where to take it and how to do it safely, in under 60 seconds.**

**Problem statement (sharpened for the challenge):**
> In Indian cities, most household e-waste never reaches a certified recycler. That isn't because people don't care. It's because they can't easily tell whether a device should be repaired, passed on or recycled, or where the nearest place is that accepts it. Old phones sit in drawers, batteries go into household bins and cause fires, and informal scrap yards burn circuit boards for metal. ReCircuit is a website that gives one clear answer and the nearest right place in under a minute.

- **Tagline:** Every old device has a next life. Find it in 60 seconds.
- **Elevator pitch (30 sec):** Billions of phones, chargers and laptops sit in drawers or end up in household bins, not because people don't care, but because the right choice is confusing. ReCircuit turns that confusion into one clear answer: a quick device check tells you whether to repair, reuse or recycle, a map shows the nearest place that accepts *that* item, and a checklist makes sure your data and batteries are handled safely. Every drop-off is tracked as real environmental impact.

## 2. The problem: "The world's fastest-growing waste stream"

**Intro paragraph:**
Every year we buy more electronics and keep them for less time. When they stop working, or simply get replaced, most of them never reach a proper recycler. They're stored and forgotten, thrown in the general bin, or sold to informal scrap dealers who burn and acid-strip them for a few grams of metal.

**Key numbers (stat tiles):**

| Number | Label |
|---|---|
| **62 million tonnes** | of e-waste generated worldwide in 2022 |
| **22.3%** | of it was formally collected and recycled |
| **82 million tonnes** | expected by 2030 if nothing changes |
| **US$62 billion** | worth of recoverable materials lost each year |

**Supporting line:** E-waste is growing about five times faster than our capacity to recycle it. *(Source: UN Global E-waste Monitor 2024)*

**Scale visual:** 62 million tonnes would fill about 1.55 million 40-tonne trucks, enough to form a bumper-to-bumper line around the equator.

## 3. Why it happens: the gap

The problem isn't that people don't care. It's that the right action is hard to find. Five things we hear again and again:

1. **"I don't know if it's worth fixing."**
   Repair is invisible. People assume a cracked screen or a dying battery means the device is finished, when it's often a cheap fix.
2. **"I don't know where it goes."**
   Existing lists of recyclers don't say *which* items each one accepts. A battery drop-off point is not the same as a TV recycler.
3. **"I'll deal with it later."**
   Unused phones and chargers pile up in drawers for years, and the valuable materials inside go with them.
4. **"The scrap dealer comes to my door."**
   Informal collection is convenient, but it often ends in open burning and acid baths that poison workers and soil.
5. **"It's just a small battery."**
   Lithium-ion batteries in vapes, power banks and earbuds thrown into ordinary bins are a growing cause of fires in garbage trucks and waste facilities.

## 4. Who it hurts

| Who | How |
|---|---|
| **Households** | Confusion, data-privacy risk from un-wiped devices, value thrown away |
| **Informal waste workers** | Exposure to lead, mercury and toxic fumes from unsafe dismantling |
| **City waste services** | Contaminated waste streams, battery fires, landfill pressure |
| **Repair shops & certified recyclers** | Hard for the people who need them to find them, so they get fewer customers and less material |
| **The planet** | More mining for new devices; critical materials like cobalt, copper and rare earths lost |

## 5. Our solution: ReCircuit

**One line:** A single place that turns "what do I do with this?" into a clear, safe, nearby action.

**Four pillars:**

1. **Decide.** A 4-question device check recommends *Repair*, *Reuse* or *Recycle*, and explains why.
2. **Locate.** A map of repair shops, donation points and certified recyclers, filtered by what they actually accept.
3. **Act safely.** A prep checklist for each device: back up, wipe your data, handle the battery.
4. **See your impact.** Every drop-off adds to your personal record of CO₂ avoided and materials recovered.

---

## 6. How it works (3 steps)

1. **Tell us about your device.** Pick what it is and how it's doing: working, broken or dead.
2. **Get your answer.** We'll tell you whether to repair, pass it on, or recycle it, and why.
3. **Drop it off.** Find the nearest place that accepts it, follow the prep checklist, and log your impact.

---

## 7. Why "repair first"?

ReCircuit follows the **waste hierarchy**. The best option is always the one that keeps a device in use longest:

**Repair → Reuse → Recycle → Dispose (last resort)**

Most of a smartphone's lifetime carbon footprint comes from *manufacturing* it, not using it. So keeping one device going for an extra year or two often does more good than recycling it perfectly. Recycling is essential, but it comes last on purpose.

# App content

## A. Device check (wizard)

**Step 1: What is it?**
Smartphone · Laptop · Tablet · Desktop / Monitor · TV · Small appliance (kettle, mixer, iron) · Large appliance (fridge, washing machine) · Chargers & cables · Batteries & power banks · Earbuds / headphones · Other

**Step 2: How is it doing?**
- *Works fine*: I just don't use it anymore
- *Works, with issues*: cracked screen, weak battery, slow
- *Doesn't work*: won't turn on or charge
- *Damaged or swollen*: bent, leaking, or the battery is bulging ⚠️

**Step 3: How old is it?**
Under 2 years · 2 to 5 years · Over 5 years · Not sure

**Step 4: Does it have your personal data on it?**
Yes · No · Not sure

## B. Decision rules

| Condition | Age | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Damaged / swollen battery | any | **Recycle, handle with care** (hazard alert shown first) |
| Works fine | under 5 yrs | **Reuse**: sell or donate |
| Works fine | over 5 yrs | **Reuse**: donate (or recycle if obsolete) |
| Works with issues | under 7 yrs | **Repair** |
| Doesn't work | under 5 yrs & repairable category | **Repair** (get a diagnosis first) |
| Doesn't work | older / not repairable | **Recycle** |
| Chargers, cables, batteries | any | **Recycle** (or reuse if working) |

If the device has personal data, the checklist always starts with backing up and wiping it.

## C. Verdict copy

**🔧 Repair it**
> Good news: this one's worth saving. Issues like this are often fixable for far less than a replacement, and keeping a device in use is the single biggest way to cut its footprint.
> **Button:** Find repair shops near me

**♻️ Pass it on**
> It still works, so it shouldn't be scrapped. Selling or donating it gives it a second life with someone who needs it, and keeps a new one from being manufactured.
> **Button:** Find donation & resale points

**🔋 Recycle it, safely**
> This device has reached the end of its road. A certified recycler can recover the copper, gold and other materials inside, and keep toxic parts out of soil and water.
> **Button:** Find certified recyclers

**⚠️ Hazard alert (swollen / damaged battery)**
> Stop: a swollen or damaged lithium battery can catch fire. Don't charge it, puncture it, or put it in any bin. Place it in a cool spot away from anything flammable and take it to a battery drop-off point as soon as you can.

## D. Prep checklists

**Phones, tablets, laptops**
1. Back up your photos, contacts and files.
2. Sign out of all accounts (Google / Apple ID, email, banking apps).
3. Turn off "Find My Device" / activation lock.
4. Factory reset the device.
5. Remove your SIM and memory card.
6. Keep the charger with it if you're donating.

**Batteries, power banks, vapes, earbuds**
1. Don't throw them in any household bin.
2. Tape over the metal terminals with clear tape.
3. Store them in a cool, dry place, away from metal objects.
4. Carry them in a non-metal container to the drop-off point.

**TVs, monitors, large appliances**
1. Unplug and let it cool for at least an hour.
2. Don't break the screen or open the back panel (older sets contain lead and mercury).
3. Book a pickup if it's too heavy to carry.
